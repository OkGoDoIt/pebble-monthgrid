The face stays in view

The face sits at the top, cut off below the first rows of days, and changes as you do. Its window is the same height on every tab, so the tabs never move. On it slides down to show the whole calendar. Tapping a part of the face opens that part’s tab. Save stays at the bottom.

Tab

  • Two lists, the way iPhone settings edit a set of controls: what’s on your watch, most important first, then More items. The minus moves an item down to More items, and the plus moves it back to the end of your list. Removing shows Undo for a few seconds.
  • Drag the handle to reorder. The red line marks where the watch runs out of room right now.
  • An arrow means there’s more. Items with options show what they’re set to; tap one and its options open under it. Items with nothing to set, like Heart rate, have no arrow.
  • No limit on items. The watch holds 8 today; the build lifts that to the whole list.
  • I left out swipe to remove: it’s hidden, it fights the drag handle on a phone, and the minus with Undo already covers it.

The next event in the month bar

The bar already picks the longest date that fits. With an event, it goes down this list and draws the first line that fits.

  1. The date as you set it, longest form first, with the whole event: OCTOBER · Standup in 12m.
  2. The same date with “in” or “at” dropped: Standup 12m.
  3. Each shorter form of the date, down to the month alone, trying both forms of the event each time.
  4. The name loses letters from its end, with an ellipsis, keeping at least three. The time is never cut.
  5. The time alone, with a calendar mark. No combination we checked needed it.
  • Placement. With an event, the date moves left and the event sits right. Without one, the date stays centered.
  • Weight and color. The date stays bold, and the event is regular. On Plain and Ruled bars, the event takes the text color.
  • The “!” for markers that couldn’t refresh keeps its spot at the right end.
  • Pebble Round 2: the room is the circle’s width where the letters sit. Pebble Time Round has no bar, so the choice isn’t offered there.
  • Quick View comes up 10 minutes before an event and shows it. If it squeezes the bar off, the event goes with it.
  • All-day events show their name only, with Any time today.

    Building it

    1. The page ships inside the watchface as one web page in place of Clay, built from these files, about 160 KB with its fonts. It sends the same settings Clay does, plus the new ones, so nothing saved today is lost.
    2. The watch learns the new settings: per-item options, battery source and threshold, the event’s place and window, and status slots for every item. The month bar’s list of lines goes into draw_calendar.c next to the one the date already uses; the own line, take-over, and phone glyph go in alongside.
    3. The two fixes: capitals for accented letters, and the phone cutting event names at a word with an ellipsis. Plus the “PM” overlap in Pixel at Large.
    4. Real values in the face: the phone already has the weather, the next event, its battery, and readings. For the watch battery, steps, and heart rate, the watch sends a snapshot when settings open; the page uses examples until it arrives.
    5. Every watch shape: the face drawing covers Pebble Time 2 today. The 144-pixel and round layouts get added the same way and checked against emulator screenshots to the pixel. Black-and-white watches skip Colors.
    6. Checks: the screenshot comparison and the month-bar check run before each release.
