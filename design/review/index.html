MonthGrid · settings page

Making MonthGrid’s settings show what they do

Three directions for the settings page, options on each status item, and four places a next event could sit on the face. Each phone below is a working prototype that draws the face with the watch’s own fonts. Try them, then tell me which parts work.

What the Pebble app lets us do

The whole page is ours

The Pebble app shows a watchface’s settings as a web page under its own App Settings bar. Below that bar we can lay out anything: pictures, a drawn face, drag to reorder. Today’s page is one such web page, built with Clay.

It can draw the face

These prototypes draw MonthGrid with a small JavaScript copy of its layout and the watch’s own font bitmaps. On 43 emulator screenshots of the Pebble Time 2 face it matches every pixel, and it draws a face in under half a millisecond. Round and older watches would each need their own layout added.

It can show your real values

The phone side already has the weather, your next event, the phone’s battery, and readings. Steps, heart rate, and the watch battery live on the watch, which would send them when settings open. The prototypes use examples.

What it costs

The page ships inside the watchface, so there’s nothing new to host, and saving works as it does now. The ongoing cost is the layout copy (about 80 KB with its fonts): it has to follow every layout change on the watch. Pictures made from the emulator at build time avoid that, but can’t show your own colors.

Three directions for the page

All three start from the status line as it is on your watch now (Phone disconnected, Battery, Next alarm, Heart rate, Weather, Phone battery, and Distance walked), with Next event added and the two batteries combined. Tap a status item for its options. The example moment is 9:37 on Thursday, with Standup at 10:00.

Pictures beside the controls

Each section opens with that part of the face, and visual choices are pictures instead of names. There’s no separate preview to scroll back to, and it’s still a plain list.

Build: simplest. It could even use pictures made ahead of time instead of the live face.

Tap the face

The face is the menu. Tap the time, the status line, the month bar, or the days, and change that part in a sheet while the face stays in view. Most direct, but every setting is hidden until you tap.

Build: needs the live face, and round watches need their own tap areas.

A day on your wrist

The status line at four moments of one day, with a mark for each item at each moment. Rules like “at 20% or less” or “2 hours before” show up as what appears when. The rest of the page is plain.

What I’d pick

The page: A, with C’s “What shows when” in its status-line section. A keeps every setting in view as a list, and each choice shows itself, so nobody has to guess what “Ruled” or “Bitham light” means. C’s marks are the clearest way to explain a line that changes through the day, which the new “Show it” rules make more important. B is the most fun to tap, but it hides every setting until you find it.

The next event: in the month bar, shown 2 hours before. It costs no height, keeps the status line’s room for your other items (four fit instead of three here), and the name stays readable. On the narrower Pebble 2 and Pebble Time screens the name would shorten first.

The items: the options in the table above. Two batteries and two calories become one item each, units and the disconnect vibration move onto their items, and Battery, Next alarm, and Next event gain a “Show it” rule.

The build: the fold line and the day marks need the face’s own measuring, so I’d ship the live face rather than pictures made ahead of time, with a test that compares it with the emulator whenever the watch layout changes.
