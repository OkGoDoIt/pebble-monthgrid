The face and its controls, together

Three ways to combine pictures beside the controls (A) with tapping the face (B), plus the rules that keep a next event readable in the month bar on every watch. Each phone is a working prototype drawn with the watch’s own fonts.

Three ways to put A and B together

They share every part and control and differ only in how you move around. From A, visual choices are drawn and each part shows its picture. From B, you can tap any part of the face, including the event. New in all three: every status item says what it’s set to and has an arrow, and tapping it opens its options in place. The example moment is 9:37 on Thursday, with Standup at 10:00.

What changed in the details

Rows show they open

Each status item shows its settings under its name, with an arrow. Tap it and the options open beneath; the arrow turns down. Remove moved in there too, so each row has one thing to tap.

Each battery on its own

Show it now offers Always, At 50% or less, and At 20% or less. With Watch and phone, each battery shows once it gets that low: a phone at 41% shows while a watch at 90% stays off.

Pictures that match the watch

A row’s picture is what the watch shows right now. An item that’s off right now shows how it looks when it’s on, dimmed. Wide pictures shrink to fit instead of being cut off.

Where the event goes is a choice

Next event’s options open with four pictures: in the month bar, on its own line, in the status line, or taking over the status line for its last 15 minutes. The month bar comes first.

The next event in the month bar

The bar already picks the longest date that fits: WEDNESDAY, OCTOBER 29, then WED, OCT 29, then OCT 29. With an event, it goes down this list and draws the first line that fits.

  1. The date as you set it, longest form first, with the whole event: OCTOBER · Standup in 23m.
  2. The same date with “in” or “at” dropped: Standup 23m.
  3. Each shorter form of the date, down to the month alone, trying both forms of the event each time. The event’s words come before the date’s length; today is still marked in the grid.
  4. The name loses letters from its end, with an ellipsis, keeping at least three. The time is never cut.
  5. The time alone, with a calendar mark. No combination we checked needed it.
  • Placement. With an event, the date moves left and the event sits right. Without one, the date stays centered, as now.
  • Weight and color. The date stays bold, and the event is regular. On Plain and Ruled bars, the event takes the text color while the date keeps the accent.
  • The “!” for markers that couldn’t refresh keeps its spot at the right end, and the event stops short of it.
  • Pebble Round 2: the room is the circle’s width where the letters sit, so the edges never clip.
  • Pebble Time Round has no bar, only a column of letters. The choice isn’t offered there; the event goes in the status line.
  • Quick View comes up 10 minutes before an event by default and shows the event itself. If it squeezes the bar into the first row of days or off the face, the event goes with the bar.
  • All-day events show their name with no time, and only with Any time today.

    What I’d pick

    The page: 3, Docked. It keeps what worked in B, since the face is always in view and changes as you tap, and it fixes what didn’t: every part has a labeled tab, so nothing has to be found by poking at the face. Each tab is short, so there’s little of A’s scrolling. If the face takes too much room on small phones, 1 is the fallback, with the same parts and controls.

    The next event: a Where setting, defaulting to the month bar, with On its own line as the second choice. Both cost the status line nothing. The two status-line choices stay for people who want everything in one row.

    Before building: the two fixes above (capitals and the phone’s cut). The bar rules then become a candidate list in draw_calendar.c, next to the one the date already uses, with this check run against it.
